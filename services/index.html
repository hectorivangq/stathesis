Services

Twelve services in four disciplines.

Each service follows the standards of the field it serves: CDC and HRSA for public health programs, CMS and NCQA for health plans, and PAHO's essential public health functions for Latin America.

I

Health Data and Evidence

Measuring population health and programs, and testing whether the numbers hold up.

I.1

Public Health and Health Insurance Statistics

Producing and interpreting population and health plan data, from surveillance, vital statistics and surveys to enrollment, claims and quality measures, with sound biostatistical methods.

For: Public health agencies, health plans and Medicaid agencies, health centers and hospitals, researchers.

Typical deliverables
  • Surveillance and epidemiologic indicators: incidence, prevalence, age-adjusted rates
  • Analysis of vital statistics and weighted population surveys
  • Enrollment, utilization and claims analysis (per member per month, rates per 1,000)
  • HEDIS, CAHPS and Medicaid quality measure calculation and validation
  • Independent statistical review of analyses prepared by others
I.2

Research and Evaluation

Systematic study of a health question or program, with quantitative, qualitative or mixed methods, that produces credible findings to guide decisions and demonstrate results.

For: Nonprofits and grantees, public health agencies, health centers and hospitals, life sciences.

Typical deliverables
  • Study protocols and evaluation plans with logic models and indicators
  • Survey design, sampling plans and questionnaires
  • Data collection and statistical analysis
  • Process and outcome evaluations of funded programs
  • Technical reports, briefs and manuscripts
I.3

Analytics Capability and Data Maturity Assessments

A structured assessment of an organization's data, systems, people and governance against a recognized maturity model, ending in a prioritized roadmap for using data better.

For: Health departments, hospitals and health systems, health plans, ministries of health.

Typical deliverables
  • Current-state review of data sources, infrastructure, governance and workforce
  • Maturity scoring against HIMSS AMAM, CDC Data Modernization or PAHO IS4H
  • Data quality and data flow review, for example EHR to UDS to state reports
  • Workforce skills inventory and training plan
  • Prioritized roadmap with milestones and costs
II

Public Health Practice

Support for the core functions health departments, ministries and their grantees answer for.

II.1

Technical Assistance in Public Health

Targeted, sustained support that helps a health agency or grantee solve a specific program, data or operational problem and build the capacity to keep solving it.

For: State, territorial and local health departments; CDC and HRSA grantees; health centers and primary care associations; ministries of health.

Typical deliverables
  • Needs and capacity assessment with a written technical assistance plan
  • Consultation and small-group coaching for program staff
  • Tools, templates and standard operating procedures
  • Training and peer-learning sessions
  • Progress measures that show what changed
II.2

Health Policy Analysis

Defining a health problem, identifying the policy options, and comparing them on health impact, feasibility, and economic and budgetary impact to support a decision.

For: Public health agencies, Medicaid agencies and health plans, nonprofits and advocates, ministries of health.

Typical deliverables
  • Problem statement and evidence review
  • Options analysis using CDC's policy analytical framework
  • Policy briefs and decision memos
  • Impact analysis of proposed bills and rules, and public comment letters
  • Stakeholder analysis and adoption strategy
II.3

Emergency Preparedness and Response

Building the plans, partnerships and tested capabilities that let health agencies and providers anticipate, respond to and recover from emergencies while keeping essential services running.

For: Public health agencies, hospitals and health care coalitions, health centers and other CMS-certified providers, ministries of health.

Typical deliverables
  • Hazard vulnerability and risk assessments
  • Emergency operations and continuity of operations plans
  • CMS Emergency Preparedness Rule compliance for providers
  • Exercise design, after-action reports and improvement plans (HSEEP)
  • Gap analysis against CDC's 15 public health preparedness capabilities
III

Health Plans and Care Delivery

How care is covered, paid for, standardized and kept within the rules.

III.1

Payer Strategy and Market Access

Securing coverage, reimbursement and contracts with public and private payers, so a product or service reaches the right patients at a price payers accept.

For: Diagnostic laboratories and pharmaceutical and device firms, providers and health centers contracting with plans, health plans, social security institutes.

Typical deliverables
  • Payer landscape analysis: commercial, Medicare Advantage and Medicaid managed care
  • Coverage and reimbursement analysis: coding, fee schedules, medical policies
  • Evidence and value materials for payer review
  • Contract strategy and negotiation support with health plans
  • Medicaid managed care contract and rate analysis
III.2

Clinical Pathways

Developing, implementing and evaluating evidence-based plans that set the sequence of care for a defined patient group, and measuring adherence and outcomes.

For: Health plans, hospitals and oncology practices, health centers, ministries of health.

Typical deliverables
  • Pathway development from clinical guidelines with clinician input
  • Pathway documents with decision points and expected outcomes
  • EHR integration through order sets and decision support
  • Adherence, variance and outcome reporting
  • Pathway use in value-based contracts and prior authorization
III.3

Regulatory Compliance

Mapping the laws and program requirements that apply to an organization, building the compliance program to meet them, and documenting that it does. This work complements legal counsel; it does not replace it.

For: Health centers, hospitals, health plans, life sciences.

Typical deliverables
  • Requirement crosswalks and gap assessments: HRSA Health Center Program, CMS Conditions of Participation, 42 CFR Parts 2 and 438
  • Compliance program review against OIG's seven elements
  • HIPAA privacy and security assessments and policies
  • HRSA Operational Site Visit readiness and corrective action plans
  • Policy manuals and staff training
IV

Programs and Systems

Designing programs, running them to plan, and building the information systems they depend on.

IV.1

Strategic Planning and Program Development

Setting priorities for an organization or community from evidence of need, then designing the programs, logic models and funding proposals that carry them out.

For: Nonprofit hospitals, health departments, health centers, nonprofits and grantees.

Typical deliverables
  • Strategic plans with goals, objectives and performance measures
  • Community health needs assessments (IRS 501(r)) and CHA/CHIP for health departments
  • Program design with logic models
  • Grant applications with budgets and evaluation plans
  • Implementation strategies and work plans
IV.2

Project Management

Applying project management knowledge, tools and techniques to plan, run and close health projects and grant-funded programs within scope, schedule, budget and funder terms.

For: Health centers and networks, public health agencies with cooperative agreements, hospitals, nonprofits and grantees.

Typical deliverables
  • Project charter, work plan and schedule
  • Risk, issue and change logs
  • Budget tracking and federal reporting, including HRSA progress reports and closeout
  • Coordination of partners, vendors and subrecipients
  • Close-out report and lessons learned
IV.3

Health Information Technology and Interoperability

Planning, selecting, optimizing and connecting the electronic systems that capture and exchange health data, so information follows the patient and supports care, reporting and analysis.

For: Health centers and networks, hospitals, public health agencies, ministries pursuing digital health.

Typical deliverables
  • EHR optimization and workflow redesign
  • Interoperability roadmaps and health information exchange onboarding (HL7 FHIR, USCDI, TEFCA)
  • UDS+ and electronic clinical quality measure readiness
  • Health IT governance, vendor selection and contracting
  • HIPAA security risk analysis, telehealth and remote patient monitoring
